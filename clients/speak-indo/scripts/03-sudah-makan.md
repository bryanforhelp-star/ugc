# Script 03 — Sudah Makan?

**Angle:** "If your Indonesian partner texts you 'sudah makan,' here's what's actually happening"
**Audience:** Foreigners dating / married to Indonesians, especially heading to meet family
**Format:** Cultural insight + vulnerable POV
**Length:** ~35 seconds (~130 words)
**Avatar:** 25–35, soft warm setting (kitchen, dining table, café), genuine — not performative. Holds phone. Could read as either gender.

## Strategy

- **Market direction:** TOWARDS (deeper connection, family acceptance) + AWAY (fear of disrespect / being the foreigner who didn't try)
- **Awareness:** Problem-aware (knows the relationship needs language effort)
- **Primary constraint:** VALUES + IDENTITY — *"I want to show I care, but I'm scared of butchering it / sounding rude"*
- **Contrarian angle:** Market view: language apps teach grammar/vocabulary. Wedge: Indonesian love is in tiny words — *sudah makan*, the phrasing of polite forms — and that's what you actually need to learn for a relationship.
- **Hook type:** Specific phrase + reveal ("they're not asking about food")

## Script

```
00:00–00:04  HOOK
VISUAL: Close-up phone screen — message bubble: "sudah makan?"
        Cut to avatar, soft warm light, holding phone.
LINE: "If your Indonesian partner keeps texting you sudah makan —
       they're not actually asking about food."

00:04–00:14  REVEAL + EMOTIONAL HEART
VISUAL: Text overlay — "sudah makan? = have you eaten?"
        Cut: Indonesian mom texting, sambal cooking, Indonesian family table.
LINE: "Literally it just means have you eaten yet. But in Indonesian culture,
       that's how love sounds. It's how moms text their kids. How partners
       check in. How you say I'm thinking about you without saying it."

00:14–00:24  CINEMATIC + CONSTRAINT DISSOLVE
VISUAL: Avatar back to camera, slightly emotional, then cut to a family
        dinner table (no faces visible — plates, hands, food, laughter).
LINE: "And if you're meeting their family, listen — you can't walk in only
       knowing terima kasih. You need maaf, saya sedang belajar — sorry,
       I'm learning. You need to say sudah back when they ask."

00:24–00:32  BRAND MENTION (vulnerable, story-embedded)
VISUAL: Phone screen — Speak Indo app, family / affection / dining decks visible.
LINE: "There's an app called Speak Indo. They have a deck just for this —
       family, affection, polite dinner table phrases. It's how I'm not
       embarrassing myself before meeting his mom in May."

00:32–00:35  PAYOFF / SOFT CLOSE (engagement loop)
VISUAL: Avatar texting "sudah" → reply with heart emoji.
LINE: "Anyway. Reply sudah next time. Watch what happens."
```

**Caption:** *Indonesian love is in the tiny words. What's the smallest phrase that hit hardest in your relationship?*
**Bio CTA:** Speak Indo — learn Bahasa in 5 min/day → speak-indo.com
**Pinned comment:** *family deck is what changed it for me — link in bio*

## Alternate hooks

1. **List-reveal:** "Three Indonesian phrases that hit harder than I love you — number two will not be on Duolingo."
2. **Direct cultural:** "The thing nobody tells you about dating an Indonesian is that the most romantic phrase in the language is literally 'have you eaten.'"
3. **POV / vulnerable:** "Meeting my Indonesian partner's parents in three weeks and I'm panic-learning Bahasa — here's what I wish I'd started with."

## Seedance / HeyGen production direction

- **HeyGen avatar:** Soft warm setting. Kitchen counter or dining table at golden hour. Genuine, slightly vulnerable energy. NOT influencer-bright. The most important script tonally — if it feels like a brand, it dies.
- **Seedance B-roll prompts** (save to `prompts/`):
  - `"close-up phone screen showing message bubble in Bahasa, soft warm window light, hand holding phone, shallow depth"`
  - `"Indonesian woman texting, kitchen, sambal cooking on stove behind her, late afternoon light, documentary feel"`
  - `"family dining table from above, hands reaching for food, plates of Indonesian dishes, no faces, candid feel, warm tungsten light"`
  - `"close-up phone with WhatsApp message reading 'sudah' and a heart emoji reply, soft natural light, cozy domestic setting"`

## Copy block velocity

- Curiosity ~25% (the reveal: "not actually about food")
- Cultural insight as proof ~20% (this IS the proof — the cultural specificity makes it credible)
- Promise ~15% (family acceptance, partner connection)
- Brand-as-mechanism ~10% (single mention, story-embedded)
- Emotional / native rhythm ~30% — highest of the three scripts on purpose

**Believability rating:** 8/10 — culturally specific, vulnerable, no over-claims. The "meeting his mom in May" detail is what sells it.
