# Speak Indo — Organic Social Scripting Style

Read this before writing or generating any script. The strategic skills (`/ad-script`, `/ad-concept`, etc.) are calibrated for direct-response. Organic short-form is a different game — apply the strategy, ditch the ad voice.

## What organic UGC is NOT

- A 30-second commercial in TikTok costume
- "Stop scrolling," "imagine if…," "what if I told you…"
- "Search Speak Indo" / "click the link in bio" hard CTAs
- Listicle formats with the brand bolted on the end
- Copywriter rhythm — punchy declarations, "here's the deal," obvious frameworks

If a script reads like a 30-second ad, it dies in the algorithm. Save no one, sell no one.

## What organic UGC IS

A **friend** telling you something interesting, embarrassing, useful, or funny — that happens to involve a product. The product earns one casual mention; the video has to stand on its own value even if no one ever clicks.

## Hard rules

1. **Hook in the first 1–2 seconds, no exceptions.** It must be a *specific* claim, confession, observation, or question — not a setup. "Confession: I've lived in Bali 18 months and only know two Indonesian words" beats "today I want to talk about learning Indonesian."

2. **Brand mentioned ONCE, casually, in the middle or near the end.** Never "Speak Indo is the best Indonesian learning app." Yes "I've been using Speak Indo for like 12 days." The product is part of *their* story, not the subject of a pitch.

3. **No hard CTA.** No "search," "click," "download." End on a vibe, a question, or an unresolved beat. The bio/caption + algorithm do the work. Examples that work:
   - "So that's something."
   - "Watch what happens."
   - "Round two was a different country."
   - "What's your worst warung moment?" (drives comments)

4. **Specifics over abstractions.** A real friend's name (Sari), a real timeframe (12 days), a real moment (the barista smiled). Generics ("locals love it when you try") sound like a brand.

5. **Native rhythm.** Sentence fragments. Talk like a person. "And the worst part?" "It's that simple." Vary sentence length — long, short, one-word.

6. **Bahasa stays Bahasa.** Drop in actual phrases (italicized in caption, said aloud) with quick gloss — *"tidak pedas — not spicy."* Don't translate everything; some friction is the point.

7. **Show, don't claim.** B-roll of the actual scenario beats a talking head describing it. Warung POV > "imagine you're at a warung."

## Format archetypes that work for Speak Indo

- **Confession** — "I've lived in Bali X months and I still…" (resident audience)
- **POV** — "POV: your Grab driver asks you something" (universal)
- **Cultural insight** — "If your partner texts you 'sudah makan,' here's what's actually happening" (dating audience)
- **Story** — "Here's the moment I realized I needed to actually learn Indonesian" (traveler/expat)
- **Mini-lesson with personality** — "Indonesian word of the day: *kepo*" (universal, low-effort, high-volume)
- **Hot take** — "Hot take: every language app sucks except for languages with 100M+ speakers" (problem aware)
- **Stitch / response** — react to a Bali content creator, an Indonesian saying something, etc.

## Format archetypes to AVOID

- Listicle ad ("30 phrases before your trip") — *unless* framed as personal story ("things I learned the hard way")
- Founder-explains-product talking head
- Voiceover + B-roll testimonial style (feels like Meta ad)
- "Top 5" anything
- "If you struggle with X, here's the solution" structure

## Length

15–45 seconds. Most should land 25–40s. Anything over 45s needs an unusually strong hook + payoff loop.

## Caption + bio

The caption does the conversion work the script refuses to:
- Caption: 1–2 sentences max + 1 strong question to drive comments + relevant hashtags (#bali #expatlife #bahasaindonesia #digitalnomad)
- Bio: "Speak Indo — learn Bahasa in 5 min/day → speak-indo.com"
- Pinned comment can be the soft CTA: *"link in bio if you want the deck"*

## Tone calibration per audience

| Audience | Tone | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Resident shame | Self-deprecating, casual, slightly funny | Don't mock — they need to feel seen, not roasted |
| Traveler survival | Helpful, story-led, slightly aspirational | Avoid tourist-board energy |
| Dating / family | Warm, vulnerable, culturally curious | Don't reduce Indonesian culture to a bit |
| Digital nomad | Observational, dry, peer-to-peer | Avoid expat-cringe lifestyle voice |

## When the strategic frameworks DO apply

The skills' core thinking is still useful:
- **Hook construction** (WHO/WHAT/HOW/CONTEXT, tangible mechanism, believability 6–8) — yes
- **Awareness level** (problem-aware vs solution-aware) — informs angle
- **Constraint to dissolve** — informs which objection the script addresses (e.g., "I'm not a language person")
- **Curiosity Quadrant** — the *contrarian angle* is what makes a hook scroll-stop

What changes is the **output** — a TikTok script, not a 60-second VSL with PAIN-PROMISE-PROOF stacked beats and a closing CTA.
