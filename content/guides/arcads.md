The button on this page is [Arcads](https://arcads.ai/?via=kyndall). That's the tool. The speed is not "make one pretty ad." It's: kit the brand, then run looks.

## the crash course

Do this once. Then stop setting up and start generating.

**1. Open Arcads.**

**2. Feed it a real-looking brand.** Site if you have one, logo file, packshot, a couple of in-the-world photos, a short brief.

**3. Pick a small set of looks.** The ones that already feel like ads you'd scroll past. List is below.

**4. Run each look across a lot of creators.** Same product. New face, new place, new first line.

**5. Keep going.** Don't stop to grade every clip. Volume first. Pull the good ones after.

That's the whole method. Arcads is what makes step 4 take minutes instead of a shoot.

## kit the brand (once)

Do this before you generate anything. If the kit is sloppy, you will make a lot of ads that all look wrong.

| Put in Arcads | What | Rule |
|---------------|------|------|
| **Site URL** | Your product page, or one page that looks like the brand exists | Paste this first if you have it. Example: [anyway](/anyway). |
| **Brief** | What it is, who it's for, three features, what it is not | Short. Steal the shape below. |
| **Logo file** | PNG or SVG of the actual wordmark | First. Not a photo of the product. Image models warp letters. |
| **Packshot** | Product on white or clean | So it knows the object. |
| **In-the-world photos** | Fridge, street, hands, desk | So it doesn't invent a studio. |

Same product. Same type. Same lighting family. Random Pinterest refs will make it look like three brands.

## the looks

These are the styles that read as real UGC, not a brand film. Make these. Then duplicate them onto new creators.

| Look | What it is | Direction to give Arcads |
|------|------------|--------------------------|
| **Sip** | Person drinking it. Flash. Night. Car, stoop, or dark room. | Phone video, direct flash, creator sipping the product, not posing for a catalog. |
| **Product to camera** | Can / box / bottle in your face. Person sharp behind it. | Hold the product toward the lens, person in focus behind it, night, street or stoop. |
| **In the wild** | Commute, walk, elevator, subway. Product in the mess of a real day. | Native social, city, keys and phone in the same hand, flash, it looks like a story. |
| **Grab** | POV hand taking it from a fridge, shelf, or bag. | First person, reaching for the product next to other real drinks or products. |
| **Desk / still** | Product sitting in a real scene. No talking. | On a table with a laptop, bag, or whatever belongs there. Night window. Not a seamless studio. |
| **Use** | Pour, open, walk with it, actually do the thing. | Hands using the product. Keep the logo readable. Don't invent a new pack. |

You do not need all six on day one. Pick three. Run them hard.

## steal the brief

Paste this shape into Arcads. The "what it is not" line is doing work. Skip it and Arcads will pretty-up your category.

```
How'd you describe your brand?

[one sentence: what it is, the vibe, what it is not]

Who is your target audience?

[who actually buys it. age, place, habit]

What are the main features?

1st: [the format]
2nd: [what's in it / what it does]
3rd: [what it looks like]
```

The one I used:

```
How'd you describe your brand?

anyway is a cold matcha latte in a clear slim can. fridge-cigarette energy. the drink you grab without thinking, not a wellness ritual.

Who is your target audience?

people who treat matcha like diet coke. 20s and 30s, city, corner store and late night, not spa.

What are the main features?

1st: ready-to-drink matcha latte, already cold
2nd: japanese matcha and oat milk
3rd: clear slim can, red type, you can see the green
```

## steal the look prompts

Once the kit is in, don't write a novel. Paste a look, generate, change the creator, generate again.

**Sip**

```
phone video, direct flash, night, creator sipping the product, looking off camera. it should look like a story, not a commercial.
```

**Product to camera**

```
creator holding the product toward the camera, product close, person in focus behind it, night, stoop or street, direct flash.
```

**In the wild**

```
native ugc, city commute, product in hand with keys or a phone, subway or sidewalk, direct flash, not looking at the camera.
```

**Grab**

```
pov, hand grabbing the product from a fridge or shelf, other real products around it, phone camera, no studio.
```

**Desk / still**

```
product on a real table at night, laptop or bag in frame, window behind it, still photo energy, not a packshot.
```

**Use**

```
hands using the product (pour, open, walk), logo stays readable, same pack as the upload, no new flavors, no new type.
```

After each one: new creator, same prompt. Then new hook / first line, same look. That is how you get a lot without starting over.

## if it looks wrong

| What you're seeing | Fix |
|--------------------|-----|
| Logo melted, extra letters, wrong color | Upload the logo file. Don't let it copy type off a product photo. |
| Spa, wellness, cafe window, pretty leaves | Brief is too soft. Add what it is not. Run again. |
| Product changes shape, flavor, or pack | Too many mixed refs. Cut back to packshot + 2 or 3 real photos. |
| Every ad is the same person | Change the creator before you change the prompt. |
| Every ad is the same idea | Change the look (sip vs grab vs commute). Don't just rewrite the caption. |
| It looks like a campaign film | Force phone camera, flash, night, not-looking-at-camera. |

## when you're done

You should have a folder that looks like a brand hired a bunch of different people, not one person on one set.

That folder is the point. Send it as a spec. Or pick variants and actually run them. Arcads got you the volume. You pick what lives.
