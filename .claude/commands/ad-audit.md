## AUDIT PROCESS

Analyze the provided copy through these 7 lenses, in order:

---

### LENS 1: AIP Structure Analysis

Does the copy follow the AIP Master Framework?

**A = ATTENTION (0-3 seconds)**
- Is there a clear hook in the first sentence?
- Which hook type is used? (WHAT / WHO / HOW / CONTEXT)
- Does it stop scrolling in 3 seconds?
- Is there a tangible mechanism or is it abstract?

**I = INTENSITY (4-15+ seconds)**
- Is there emotional escalation?
- For AWAY markets: Is there a Pain Chain? (General -> Specific -> Cinematic -> Emotional -> Core Wound)
- For TOWARDS markets: Is there a Promise Ladder? (General -> Specific -> Cinematic -> Emotional -> Transformation)
- Are constraints dissolved using A.W.E. Formula? (Acknowledge, Wedge, Elaborate)
- Is proof woven immediately after claims?

**P = POSSIBILITY (Final 5-10+ seconds)**
- Is there a clear promise escalation?
- Is there a proof stack?
- Are final constraints removed?
- Is there urgency/scarcity (when appropriate)?
- Is the CTA clear and specific?

**AIP Score: [X]/10**

### LENS 2: Copy Block Velocity

Analyze every sentence and tag which Copy Block it serves:

- **PAIN** - Problem they're experiencing now (problem tokens, daily symptoms)
- **PROMISE** - Specific desirable benefits (current reality -> ideal state)
- **CURIOSITY** - Pattern interrupts (hidden causes, unique mechanisms, visual surprises)
- **PROOF** - Permission to believe (credible, social, psychological, experiential, empirical)
- **CONSTRAINTS** - Dissolving perceived limitations (identity, values, beliefs, experience, resources)

Count total words. Count words serving a Copy Block. Calculate percentage.

**Target: 90-95% of words must serve a Copy Block.**

Flag any filler sentences - words that don't serve Pain, Promise, Curiosity, Proof, or Constraints.

- Promise: [X]%
- Curiosity: [X]%
- Proof: [X]%
- Constraints: [X]%
- Filler: [X]%

**Copy Velocity Score: [X]%**

### LENS 3: C.R.A.V.E.S. Quality Check

Evaluate each major Copy Block sentence against:

- **C = Clear** - Instantly understandable? Or requires mental processing?
- **R = Relevant** - Directly applicable to the target audience?
- **A = Accurate** - Precisely true? No fabricated or exaggerated claims?
- **V = Visual** - Creates a mental movie? Can they picture it?
- **E = Expressive** - Emotionally charged language? Or flat and neutral?
- **S = Specific** - Concrete details? Or vague generalities?

Flag any sentence that fails 2+ dimensions.

**C.R.A.V.E.S. Score: [X]/10**

---

### LENS 4: Hook Testing Matrix

Evaluate the opening hook against 9 filters:

1. **Scroll Test**: Would this make someone stop scrolling? (Y/N)
2. **3-Second Rule**: Understandable in 3 seconds or less? (Y/N)
3. **Curiosity Gap**: Creates an information gap demanding resolution? (Y/N)
4. **Relevance Check**: Immediately clear why this matters to THEM? (Y/N)
5. **Believability Scale**: Hits 6-8? (score it 1-10)
6. **Specificity Audit**: Could be more specific without losing believability? (Y/N)
7. **Pattern Interrupt**: Violates market expectations? (Y/N)
8. **Personal Stakes**: Makes viewer think "This is about ME"? (Y/N)
9. **Click Test**: Would you personally click this? (Y/N)

**Hook Matrix Score: [X]/9 YES answers**

---

### LENS 5: Human Language Check

Does this sound like a real person talking?

Check for:
- [ ] Varied sentence structures (long, medium, short)?
- [ ] Sentences starting with And, But, So?
- [ ] Contractions and verbal tics ("Look," "Here's the thing")?
- [ ] Natural transitions and pattern interrupts?
- [ ] One thought per sentence?
- [ ] Conversational rhythm (would someone actually say this out loud)?

**Red Flags:**
- Corporate/marketing speak ("leverage", "optimize", "synergy")
- Robotic sentence structure (all same length)
- No contractions (sounds stiff)
- Multiple ideas crammed into one sentence
- Transition phrases that feel scripted ("But wait, there's more!")

**Human Language Score: [X]/10**

---

### LENS 6: Market Calibration Check

Is the copy calibrated correctly for:

**Market Direction:**
- TOWARDS market -> Should lead with PROMISE + CURIOSITY
- AWAY market -> Should lead with PAIN + PROOF
- Is it leading with the right blocks?

**Awareness Level:**
- Unaware -> Heavy PAIN
- Problem Aware -> PAIN + CURIOSITY
- Solution Aware -> CURIOSITY + PROMISE
- Product Aware -> PROMISE + PROOF
- Is the awareness level matched correctly?

**Industry-Specific Constraints:**
- Are the right constraint types being addressed?
- Are proof types appropriate for this market? (social vs authority vs empirical)
- Any compliance issues? (health claims, income claims, etc.)

**Market Calibration Score: [X]/10**

---

### LENS 7: Proof Architecture

How is proof deployed?

**Proof Types Present:**
- [ ] Psychological Proof (testable concepts, transparency)
- [ ] Experiential Proof (demos, before/after, "watch what happens")
- [ ] Empirical Proof (studies, research, data)
- [ ] Credible Proof (expert/institutional backing)
- [ ] Social Proof (relatable people getting results)

**Proof Placement:**
- Is proof placed immediately after claims? (Y/N)
- Are multiple proof types braided together? (Y/N)
- Is the proof believable and specific? (Y/N)

**Proof Architecture Score: [X]/10**

## DIAGNOSIS & PRESCRIPTION

After scoring, provide:

### Top 3 Strengths
What's working well in this copy and why.

### Top 3 Critical Issues
The biggest problems ranked by impact on conversion, with specific explanations.

### Specific Rewrites
For each critical issue, provide a BEFORE/AFTER rewrite showing exactly how to fix it. Include which framework principle the fix applies.

### Quick Wins
2-3 small changes that would immediately improve performance without a full rewrite.

### Strategic Recommendations
Any higher-level strategic issues (wrong market direction, wrong awareness level, missing proof type, etc.)

---

## CRITICAL RULES

1. Be brutally honest. Flattery doesn't improve conversion rates.
2. Every criticism must include a specific fix with a rewrite example.
3. Score based on the frameworks, not personal taste.
4. Flag any fabricated/unverifiable claims as compliance risks.
5. If the copy is strong, say so - don't manufacture issues.
6. Always end with the single highest-impact change they should make first.
